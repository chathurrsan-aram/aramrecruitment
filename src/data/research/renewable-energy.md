## Why Now

### Macro Tailwind - Diaspora Remittance Trends

Sri Lanka's worker remittances have staged one of the most dramatic recoveries in South Asian economic history: surging from a crisis-low of $3.8 billion in 2022 to a projected ~$7.4 billion in 2025, with monthly inflows hitting an all-time record of $879 million in December 2025. Critically, the composition of these flows has shifted. North America-origin remittances - predominantly from Tamil diaspora hubs in Canada and the United States - grew approximately 200% between 2023–2024, and 50% of all remittances now originate outside the Middle East. This means the Tamil diaspora, historically the largest source of remittance capital to Northern and Eastern provinces, is generating unprecedented investable surplus at precisely the moment when Sri Lanka's 70% renewable-by-2030 mandate is channelling billions in energy infrastructure investment into these same Tamil-majority regions. The opportunity is to structure diaspora capital flows - currently consumed or held in savings - into productive renewable energy assets.

### Data Point 1: Remittance Surge and Tamil Diaspora Corridors

Sri Lanka's remittances surged from $3.8B (2022) to $6.58B (2024), with North America/Tamil diaspora corridors growing ~200%; monthly inflows hit an all-time record of $879M in December 2025, putting 2025 on track for ~$7.4B - the highest in over a decade.

The post-crisis shift in remittance corridors is structurally significant: 50% of flows now originate outside the Middle East, reflecting the growing economic weight of the Tamil diaspora in Canada, the UK, and Australia. This capital is currently flowing primarily into consumption and savings; structuring it into renewable energy investment vehicles represents a major untapped opportunity.

(Sources: Central Bank of Sri Lanka monthly remittance data; EconomyNext, January and April 2025; Macrocolombo analysis citing MOF Annual Report 2024)

### Data Point 2: Mannar Wind Corridor - Operational and Expanding

The Mannar Wind Corridor in Tamil-majority Northern Province is already operational at 103.5 MW (producing 345,600 MWh/year at under 5¢/kWh), with a 300+ MW expansion pipeline including $500M in proposed investment - anchoring one of South Asia's best wind resources.

The Thambapawani wind farm, commissioned December 2020 on Mannar Island with $200 million ADB financing, avoids 265,700 tons of CO₂ annually. The total Mannar Wind Power Park is planned for 300 MW, with:
- A proposed Phase 2 of 250 MW (Adani Green Energy, ~$500M) - though this was subsequently impacted by Adani's withdrawal
- A BOI-listed 100 MW Phase II
- Envision breaking ground on an additional 50 MW project in March 2026

This makes Mannar district one of the most active renewable energy investment corridors in South Asia.

(Sources: ADB Project #49345-002; Ceylon Electricity Board; BOI Sri Lanka; Renewable Energy Asia Portal, March 2026)

### Data Point 3: 70% Renewable Target and $6B Investment Requirement

Sri Lanka's Cabinet-approved 70% renewable target by 2030 requires 5.8 GW of new capacity and an estimated $6 billion in infrastructure investment concentrated in Northern/Eastern provinces, with the World Bank approving $150M (June 2025) to mobilise $800M+ in private capital.

The build-out plan comprises:
- **4,705 MW** solar
- **1,825 MW** wind
- **195 MW** mini-hydro
- **200 MW** biomass

Northern and Eastern provinces are designated as primary renewable energy zones including 9 backbone transmission lines. The World Bank programme includes IFC direct investments and MIGA political risk insurance to de-risk private participation. Sri Lanka's renewable share already hit 70% in June 2025 (CEB data), up from 48.3% a year prior, demonstrating rapid momentum.

(Sources: Public Utilities Commission of Sri Lanka; Ministry of Power & Energy, May 2024; World Bank Press Release, June 2025; EconomyNext, August 2025)

### Opportunity Window

The post-crisis remittance surge - from $3.8B (2022) to a projected $7.4B (2025), with Tamil diaspora corridors in North America growing ~200% - is generating unprecedented investable capital at precisely the moment Sri Lanka's 70% renewable-by-2030 mandate channels $6B+ in infrastructure investment into Tamil-majority Northern and Eastern provinces, where the operational Mannar wind corridor already demonstrates commercial viability at under 5¢/kWh. The convergence of World Bank/IFC/ADB de-risking instruments ($150M programme mobilising $800M+ in private capital with MIGA political risk insurance), BOI renewable energy incentives, and surging formal remittance flows creates a narrow but powerful window to structure diaspora-backed renewable energy investment vehicles - channelling remittance capital from consumption into productive energy assets in the very regions where it originates.

## Deep Context

### Government Targets - Full Detail

Sri Lanka's government targets 70% electricity from renewables by 2030 and carbon neutrality by 2050, with plans to add 5.8 GW of new renewable capacity between 2023 and 2030. The Northern Province hosts the country's prime renewable resources for both wind and solar.

### Mannar Wind Corridor - Complete Data

- **Average wind speeds**: 8–9 m/s - world-class wind resource
- **Plant factor**: 40%+ - world-class performance
- **Cost per kWh**: Under 5 US cents - less than a third the cost of fossil generation
- **Operational capacity**: 103.5 MW Thambapavani wind farm (CEB/ADB, funded with $141 million CEB commitment and $200 million ADB financing)
- **Annual production**: 345,600 MWh/year
- **CO₂ avoided**: 265,700 tons annually
- **Commissioned**: December 2020 on Mannar Island
- **Total Mannar Wind Power Park planned**: 300 MW
- **Phase 2 proposed**: 250 MW (Adani Green Energy, ~$500M) - impacted by Adani's February 2025 withdrawal
- **BOI-listed**: 100 MW Phase II
- **Envision**: Breaking ground on 50 MW project in March 2026
- **Total expansion pipeline**: 300+ MW with $500M+ in proposed investment

### Pooneryn 1,040 MW Hybrid Park - Full Detail

The Pooneryn Wind-Solar Hybrid Energy Park in Kilinochchi district has received cabinet approval for up to **1,040 MW of combined wind and solar capacity**. This is one of the largest planned hybrid renewable energy projects in South Asia and would transform the Northern Province's energy infrastructure. Located in Kilinochchi - one of the most war-affected and economically deprived districts - the project represents a potential anchor for regional economic development.

### 70% Renewable Generation Milestone (June 2025) - Full Detail

In June 2025, CEB recorded **70% renewable generation for the first time** - hitting the 2030 target five years early on a monthly basis. This was up from 48.3% renewable share just a year prior, demonstrating extraordinarily rapid momentum. While this was a single month's achievement rather than sustained annual performance, it demonstrated the technical feasibility of high-renewable grid operation in Sri Lanka.

### Solar Resources - Full Detail

Northern and Eastern provinces register the highest solar irradiation in the country at **1,766–2,106 kWh/m² GHI** (Global Horizontal Irradiance). Combined with the Mannar wind corridor, this makes the Tamil-majority regions the epicentre of Sri Lanka's renewable energy future.

### Adani Withdrawal Context - Full Detail

Adani withdrew its **484 MW, $442 million** wind project in **February 2025** over tariff disputes with the new NPP government. The withdrawal highlighted:
- Contract renegotiation risk under new political administrations
- The importance of tariff certainty for large-scale renewable investment
- The vulnerability of foreign-led mega-projects to political dynamics
- A potential opening for smaller-scale, community-aligned diaspora-backed projects

### HyWind Suspension - Full Detail

Tamil community protests **suspended HyWind One's 50 MW project in August 2025** over land rights and consent concerns. This is a critical case study demonstrating that:
- Community consent is now a deal-critical factor in Northern Province
- Projects that proceed without genuine local engagement face material execution risk
- The Tamil community is actively exercising agency over development decisions in their regions
- Diaspora-backed projects with authentic community relationships may have a structural advantage over external developers

### Grid Infrastructure - Full Detail

Grid infrastructure in the North requires **complete upgrading** to support commercial-scale deployment. The national build-out plan includes **9 backbone transmission lines** with Northern and Eastern provinces designated as primary renewable energy zones. Grid modernisation is both a constraint on and an investment opportunity in the renewable energy sector.

### India-Sri Lanka HVDC Interconnection - Full Detail

The proposed India–Sri Lanka HVDC (High Voltage Direct Current) interconnection would run from **Madurai to Anuradhapura via the Palk Strait**. The project is in advanced study stages with ADB involvement. If completed, it would:
- Enable power trading between India and Sri Lanka
- Provide grid stability for Northern Province renewable generation
- Create a physical energy connection between Tamil Nadu and Tamil-majority Sri Lanka
- Potentially allow surplus Northern Province renewable generation to be exported to India

### ADB/World Bank Financing - Complete Breakdown

- **World Bank**: $150 million energy programme (June 2025), expected to mobilise $800 million+ in private investment through IFC financing and MIGA political risk insurance
- **ADB total**: $430 million+ committed across wind, grid strengthening, and battery storage projects
- **ADB Thambapawani wind farm**: $200 million financing (part of ADB Project #49345-002)
- **IFC**: Direct investments in renewable projects as part of the World Bank programme
- **MIGA**: Political risk insurance to de-risk private participation - critical for addressing the trust deficit

### GREAT 2025–2030 Battery Storage Targets - Full Detail

Sri Lanka's GREAT 2025–2030 plan targets **650 MW / 2,250 MWh** of battery energy storage. Battery storage is a critical enabler for intermittent renewable generation (wind and solar) and represents a significant investment opportunity in its own right. Without adequate storage, the grid cannot absorb the planned 5.8 GW of new renewable capacity.

### Capacity Build-Out Plan (2023–2030) - Complete Detail

The national renewable energy build-out plan comprises:
- **4,705 MW** solar
- **1,825 MW** wind
- **195 MW** mini-hydro
- **200 MW** biomass
- **650 MW / 2,250 MWh** battery energy storage (GREAT plan)
- **Total new capacity needed**: 5.8 GW
- **Estimated total investment**: $6 billion
- **Primary zones**: Northern and Eastern provinces
- **Grid**: 9 backbone transmission lines

## Investment Thesis Notes

Renewable energy is identified as a **medium-term higher-impact play** requiring either larger capital or co-investment structures with DFIs. It is not in the near-term priority bucket due to capital intensity and execution complexity.

The Mannar wind corridor demonstrates commercial viability at world-class economics (40%+ plant factor, under 5¢/kWh, less than a third of fossil generation costs). The convergence of government targets (5.8 GW by 2030, $6B investment), DFI de-risking instruments ($150M mobilising $800M+, MIGA insurance), and surging diaspora remittances ($7.4B projected) creates a structural opportunity.

However, material constraints must be acknowledged:
- **Community consent risk**: Adani ($442M) and HyWind (50 MW) demonstrate this is not theoretical
- **Grid infrastructure gaps**: The North requires complete grid upgrading
- **Capital intensity**: Ticket sizes of £250K+ institutional; co-investment with ADB/IFC is the most realistic entry mode for diaspora capital
- **Contract renegotiation risk**: Under the new NPP government

Battery storage (650 MW / 2,250 MWh GREAT target) is a parallel opportunity that may be more accessible for smaller-scale diaspora investment.

**No diaspora-led energy initiatives have been identified** - a clear gap that aligns with the broader structural finding about the absence of a diaspora investment vehicle.
