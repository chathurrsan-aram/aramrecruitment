## Deep Context

### Historic Catch Data — Full Detail

Before the civil war, Northern Province contributed **40% of national marine fish catch** — **75,740 tons in 1983**. This was the dominant fishing region in Sri Lanka, reflecting the Northern Province's extensive coastline and marine resources.

Production collapsed to just **4,500 tons** at the conflict's nadir — a devastating destruction of the fishing economy that sustained coastal Tamil communities. The collapse reflected the physical destruction of boats, harbours, and equipment; the displacement of fishing communities; and military restrictions on fishing.

Production recovered to **75,470 tons** by 2016 — essentially matching the pre-war tonnage — but this now represents only **16.5% of national output**, less than half its historic 40% share. The catch volume recovered but the competitive position did not, because other provinces developed their fishing infrastructure during the decades of Northern Province destruction.

### The Zero Fishery Harbours Finding — Full Detail

The single most critical constraint on Northern Province fisheries: Sri Lanka has **21 operational fishery harbours** nationally, and the Northern Province has **zero**.

This is an extraordinary infrastructure gap. Despite having one of Sri Lanka's longest coastlines and historically contributing 40% of the national catch, the Northern Province has no single fishery harbour — meaning fish must be landed at informal sites with no cold storage, processing, or quality control infrastructure. This directly drives the extreme post-harvest losses and quality degradation documented below.

### Post-Harvest Loss Rates — Full Detail

- Post-harvest losses in offshore fisheries reach **40–60%**
- Only **16% of yellowfin tuna** meets exportable quality
- These losses are directly attributable to the lack of harbour infrastructure, cold chain, and processing capacity
- The overlap with the AgriTech cold chain thesis is significant — cold chain infrastructure serves both agricultural and fisheries sectors

### ADB $62M Project — Complete Detail

ADB's **$62 million Northern Province Sustainable Fisheries Development Project** will develop:
- **Harbours** at three locations: **Point Pedro**, **Gurunagar**, and **Pesalai**
- **7 anchorages** across the Northern Province coastline
- **21 landing sites** providing formal fish landing infrastructure
- **Sri Lanka's first seaweed tissue culture laboratory** — to be located in **Jaffna**

This project is transformational: it creates the first fishery harbour infrastructure in Northern Province history since the war. The three harbour locations (Point Pedro on the peninsula's north coast, Gurunagar near Jaffna city, Pesalai on Mannar Island) provide geographic coverage across the province's key fishing zones.

### National Fisheries Sector — Full Detail

The national fisheries sector supports **2.7 million people** — **18% of the population** including indirect dependents. Seafood exports reached approximately **$285 million** in 2024. Fisheries is therefore both an economic and a social sector — investment affects a significant share of the Tamil-majority population.

### Aquaculture Data — Complete Detail

Aquaculture presents the highest-growth opportunities in the fisheries sector:

**Shrimp production**:
- Government targets **100,000 metric tons** of shrimp production by **2027**
- Current production approximately **~11,000 MT** in 2022 (but surging rapidly)
- Production surged from **543 MT (2019) to ~16,000 MT** after government approval of **L. vannamei** species — a **30x growth** in approximately two years
- L. vannamei introduction since 2019 has transformed the industry
- Fisheries mega-zones designated in **Ampara** and **Kilinochchi**

**Land utilisation**:
- NAQDA identified **8,500+ hectares** suitable for aquaculture — **only 25% utilised**
- An additional **100,000 hectares** of shallow marine water from **Mannar to the North** suitable for farming
- The 75% unutilised aquaculture land represents one of the largest untapped productive assets in Tamil-majority regions

### Seaweed Cultivation Economics — Full Detail

Seaweed cultivation in Mannar and Kilinochchi has been **commercially active since 2012** and represents a proven, low-capital livelihood model:

- Generates **$465–615/month per family** from **25 rafts**
- This income level is significant in the Northern Province context (where daily wages in other sectors are $5–6/day)
- ADB's $62M project includes Sri Lanka's first seaweed tissue culture laboratory in Jaffna — which will improve seed stock quality and potentially increase yields
- Seaweed cultivation requires minimal capital investment and can be operated by families, making it highly suitable for community-level diaspora investment

### Sea Cucumber Export Surge — Full Detail

Sea cucumber exports surged from **$2.3 million (2016) to $15.7 million (2019)** — a nearly 7x increase in three years. The Chinese market demand is described as "insatiable" — sea cucumber is a high-value delicacy in Chinese cuisine with growing demand as Chinese middle-class consumption expands. This export trajectory demonstrates the potential for high-value marine product exports from Northern Province waters once infrastructure (harbours, processing) is in place.

### Taprobane Seafood — Full Detail

**Taprobane Seafood** is Sri Lanka's largest seafood company. Key details:
- Operates **mini processing plants in Jaffna** — one of the few significant private sector employers in Northern Province
- **Directly employs war-affected communities** — making it both an economic and social enterprise
- On a trajectory toward **$200 million revenue**
- Demonstrates that commercial-scale seafood operations in the Northern Province are viable and can be structured to deliver social impact alongside financial returns

## Key Organisations & Projects

- **ADB Northern Province Sustainable Fisheries Development Project** — $62 million. Will develop harbours at Point Pedro, Gurunagar, and Pesalai; 7 anchorages; 21 landing sites; Sri Lanka's first seaweed tissue culture laboratory in Jaffna. Transformational infrastructure that creates the preconditions for private investment in processing, cold chain, and export.
- **Taprobane Seafood** — Sri Lanka's largest seafood company. Mini processing plants in Jaffna. Directly employs war-affected communities. Trajectory toward $200M revenue. Demonstrates commercial viability of Northern Province seafood operations.
- **NAQDA (National Aquaculture Development Authority)** — Identified 8,500+ hectares suitable for aquaculture (only 25% utilised); 100,000 hectares of shallow marine water from Mannar to the North suitable for farming. The government body responsible for aquaculture development — its land surveys identify the scale of untapped opportunity.
- **HALO Trust** — Demining has enabled return of coastal fishing communities. Cleared 300,000 mines, returned 120+ km² to communities. Ongoing demining in 2025.
- **Government of Sri Lanka** — Set 100,000 MT shrimp production target for 2027. Approved L. vannamei species introduction. Designated fisheries mega-zones in Ampara and Kilinochchi.

## Investment Thesis Notes

Aquaculture is identified as one of the **four highest-priority near-term sectors** alongside cold chain logistics, ethical fintech, and specialist healthcare. The investment case rests on several converging factors:

**Proven commercial traction**: The 30x shrimp production growth in two years (543 MT to ~16,000 MT) after L. vannamei introduction demonstrates that the aquaculture opportunity is real and already materialising. Sea cucumber exports surging from $2.3M to $15.7M (2016–2019) show export market demand. Taprobane Seafood's trajectory toward $200M revenue proves commercial-scale operations work.

**Massive untapped supply**: 75% of NAQDA-identified aquaculture land is unutilised. 100,000 hectares of shallow marine water from Mannar to the North is suitable for farming. These figures represent one of the largest untapped productive marine assets in South Asia.

**Infrastructure catalyst**: ADB's $62M project creates the first Northern Province harbours and processing infrastructure, unlocking private investment in the value chain above.

**Accessible entry points**: Seaweed cultivation at $465–615/month per family from 25 rafts offers a low-ticket-size, high-social-impact entry point. Aquaculture operations and cold storage at £50–250K provide mid-range options.

**Cold chain overlap**: The cold chain infrastructure needed for fisheries overlaps directly with the AgriTech cold chain thesis — a single cold chain investment can serve both agricultural and marine products. Post-harvest losses of 40–60% in offshore fisheries match the agricultural post-harvest loss crisis, amplifying the case for integrated cold chain development.

**Export orientation**: Shrimp, sea cucumber, and seaweed are primarily export products with established international markets, reducing dependence on the small domestic Sri Lankan market.
