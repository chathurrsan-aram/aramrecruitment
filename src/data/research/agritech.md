## Why Now

### Macro Tailwind - Post-2022 Economic Crisis Recovery

Sri Lanka's 2022 economic crisis - marked by a 7.8% GDP contraction, sovereign default, and the devastating aftermath of the 2021–2022 organic fertiliser mandate - shattered agricultural productivity across the island. Paddy yields collapsed from 4.6 MT/ha (2020/21) to 3.6 MT/ha, and food insecurity spiked to crisis levels. The recovery now underway is not a return to the status quo: it is a structural reset. Farmers, policymakers, and multilateral institutions have collectively abandoned the pre-crisis model of blanket input subsidisation in favour of precision agriculture, digital advisory, and climate-smart practices. This shift creates a once-in-a-generation entry point for AgriTech ventures, particularly in the Tamil-majority Northern and Eastern provinces where food insecurity remains most acute and where unprecedented multilateral capital is now explicitly earmarked.

### Data Point 1: Persistent Productivity Gap

Paddy production rebounded 33% (2022–2023) but yields remain ~9% below pre-crisis levels, signalling a persistent productivity gap that AgriTech can close. Sri Lanka's paddy output surged from 3.39 MMT in 2022 to 4.51 MMT in 2023, with USDA FAS forecasting 5.02 MMT for MY 2024/25 - still below the pre-ban peak of ~5.1 MMT. Yields have recovered to only 4.2 MT/ha versus the pre-crisis 4.6 MT/ha.

The USAID-FAO $9.2 million agricultural revitalisation partnership (announced 2023) specifically targets Vavuniya district (Northern Province) as one of four project districts, aiming to help 10,000 farmers improve productivity.

(Sources: Central Bank of Sri Lanka Agriculture Sector Data Summary, June 2024; USDA Foreign Agricultural Service Grain and Feed Annual 2024; Ministry of Agriculture/HEARTI survey 2023)

### Data Point 2: Food Insecurity Concentrated in Tamil Districts

Five of Sri Lanka's six most food-insecure districts are Tamil-majority Northern/Eastern districts, with 24% of all households still food insecure as of late 2023. The FAO/WFP Crop and Food Security Assessment Mission (Feb–March 2023) identified Kilinochchi, Mannar, Batticaloa, Vavuniya, and Jaffna among the most food-insecure districts nationally. A subsequent WFP survey (Aug–Sep 2023) found approximately 5.3 million people remained food insecure, with widespread needs concentrated in Northern and Eastern provinces. Drought conditions in August 2023 hit Jaffna, Ampara, and Batticaloa hardest, accounting for 85% of the ~150,000 people affected.

(Sources: FAO/WFP Special Report - CFSAM to Sri Lanka, May 2023; WFP Household Food Security Overview 2023, published May 2024)

### Data Point 3: $1.3B+ in Multilateral Capital with AgriTech Co-Investment Mandates

Over $1.3 billion in multilateral capital committed to agriculture and North/East regional development (2024–2028), with explicit AgriTech co-investment mandates:

- The World Bank's May 2025 $1 billion package includes $100 million for agriculture technology adoption (targeting 380,000+ beneficiaries, 8,000 agri-food producers, leveraging $17M in private financing) and $200 million specifically for Northern and Eastern Province development.
- The ADB approved $200 million (December 2025) for precision irrigation infrastructure including SCADA systems and on-farm drip irrigation.
- Sri Lanka currently has only ~40 AgriTech startups (Tracxn, January 2026), of which just 4 are funded - indicating a dramatically underserved market relative to capital inflows.

(Sources: World Bank Press Releases, May and September 2025; ADB News Release, December 2025; GoviLab AgriTech Accelerator/IDAT strategy backed by Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation, January 2024)

### Opportunity Window

Sri Lanka's post-crisis agricultural reset - where paddy yields remain 9% below pre-crisis levels and food insecurity is concentrated in Tamil-majority Northern and Eastern districts - has triggered over $1.3 billion in multilateral capital explicitly targeting technology-enabled farming and North/East regional development. With only ~40 modestly funded AgriTech startups serving 1.2 million smallholder families, early movers can capture outsized market share at precisely the moment when institutional de-risking capital is actively seeking private-sector co-investment partners.

## Deep Context

### Agriculture in Northern Province - Structural Overview

Agriculture employs over 85% of Northern Province residents yet receives just 2.4% of public investment in Jaffna district. The national agriculture sector contributes $8.0–8.3 billion (8.3% of GDP), but Northern Province per capita income sits at roughly half the national average ($810 vs. $1,610). The Northern Province faces existential challenges in its agricultural base.

### Post-Harvest Loss Crisis - Full Detail

Post-harvest losses of 20–40% for vegetables and 30–40% for fruits - totalling approximately 580,000 metric tons and approximately $90 million annually in fruit alone - stem from virtually zero cold chain infrastructure in the North and East. The total figure across all produce is approximately **$200 million annually** in destroyed value.

Sri Lanka lacks integrated cold-chain infrastructure; most produce travels in open trucks. Only 1% of fresh produce flows through supermarket channels. Supply chain inefficiency destroys enormous value at every stage from farm to market.

Farmers in the North face compounded disadvantage: the A9 highway to Jaffna was only recently restored, rail links were rebuilt with Indian assistance in 2015, and middleman exploitation is extreme - as one illustration, Nuwara Eliya farmers must sell "four kilograms of carrots" to afford "purchasing a kilogram of rice."

Post-harvest losses in offshore fisheries are even higher at 40–60%, and only 16% of yellowfin tuna meets exportable quality - representing overlap with the cold chain thesis in the fisheries sector.

### Palmyrah Industry - Full Detail

Jaffna's 115 million palmyrah trees - 95% of Sri Lanka's total - support 12,000+ workers and produce approximately 800 products including toddy, jaggery, odiyal flour, fibre, and timber. The industry remains artisanal and fragmented despite its scale. Palmyrah product exports surged **322% year-on-year** (from Rs. 22M to Rs. 93M in Jan–Feb 2025), demonstrating latent global demand when supply chains function.

### Groundwater and Salinity Crisis - Full Detail

43% of Jaffna's paddy land - approximately 8,178 hectares - has been **permanently abandoned** due to groundwater salinity. Salinity increased 1.6-fold over two decades as over-extraction from the peninsula's 19,198 agro-wells exceeded aquifer recharge rates. This is not a reversible problem - it represents permanent loss of agricultural capacity that makes the remaining productive land even more critical to optimise through technology.

### Fertiliser Ban Aftermath - Full Detail

The 2021 organic fertiliser mandate compounded agricultural damage across the entire country:
- Rice production fell **20% nationally**
- Tea yields dropped **26.8%**
- **54% of farmers** lost over half their harvest
- Paddy yields collapsed from 4.6 MT/ha (2020/21) to 3.6 MT/ha
- Food insecurity spiked to crisis levels

The recovery has been structural rather than a return to the old model - the institutional consensus has shifted permanently toward precision agriculture and technology-enabled farming.

### Eastern Province - "Granary of Sri Lanka" - Full Detail

The Eastern Province is known as the "Granary of Sri Lanka" and contributes:
- **25%** of national paddy production
- **17%** of milk
- **21%** of fish

Trincomalee's natural harbour - one of the world's largest - anchors an India-Sri Lanka-UAE trilateral energy hub agreement signed in April 2025. Fisheries mega-zones have been designated in Ampara and Kilinochchi. The Eastern Province represents significant agricultural, aquaculture, and logistics potential that is distinct from the Northern Province profile.

### High-Value Crop Opportunities - Full Detail

- **Palmyrah products**: From Jaffna's 115 million trees (95% of national total), supporting 12,000+ workers, ~800 products. Exports surged 322% YoY in early 2025 (Rs. 22M to Rs. 93M)
- **Moringa**: Cultivated in North Central Province, exported organically to Europe. Growing global demand for moringa as a superfood creates direct diaspora market connection
- **Ceylon cinnamon**: 85% global true-cinnamon market share, $232M export value. Sri Lanka's dominance in true cinnamon (as opposed to cassia) is a natural premium positioning
- **Tea exports**: Reached $1.51 billion in 2025; global specialty tea market projected to hit $10.75 billion by 2033 at 6% CAGR. Smallholders produce 75% of Sri Lanka's tea (see Hill Country sector for full analysis)
- **Spice and concentrate exports**: $461.85 million in 2024 (up 16.11% YoY)

All represent high-value crop opportunities with direct diaspora market connections to Canada, UK, and Australia. The Tamil diaspora in these countries represents both a consumer market and an investment base for value-added processing.

### KKS Port Rehabilitation - Full Detail

KKS Port's rehabilitation is funded by a $45.27 million Indian Exim Bank credit line. Once complete, it will dramatically reduce transit times for Northern agricultural exports to India's 1.4 billion consumers. The port creates a natural logistics hub for cold chain and agricultural export infrastructure in the Northern Province.

### Demining Impact on Agriculture - Full Detail

HALO Trust cleared 300,000 anti-personnel mines and returned 120+ km² of land to communities, enabling 280,000 displaced people to return to their homes and farms. Contamination fell from 506 km² (2010) to 25.8 km² (2018), though mines are still being discovered in 2025. The demining process has been transformational for agricultural recovery - without it, much of the Northern Province's farmland would remain inaccessible.

### Multilateral Capital - Detailed Breakdown

- **World Bank ASMP (Agriculture Sector Modernization Project)**: $89 million deployed across Jaffna, Mullaitivu, Kilinochchi, Vavuniya, and Batticaloa - established technology parks across five Tamil-majority districts
- **ADB Mahaweli Water Security Investment**: $200 million; benefits 35,600 farming households in North Central Province
- **ADB Precision Irrigation**: $200 million (approved December 2025) for SCADA systems and on-farm drip irrigation infrastructure
- **USAID-FAO Partnership**: $9.2 million agricultural revitalisation targeting Vavuniya district, 10,000 farmers
- **World Bank May 2025 Package**: $100M for agriculture technology adoption (380,000+ beneficiaries, 8,000 agri-food producers, $17M private financing leverage); $200M for N/E development
- **GoviLab AgriTech Accelerator**: Partnership between Hatch, Sarvodaya, and the Bill & Melinda Gates Foundation. First cohort of 10 agritech startups (2024) with solutions including drones for paddy cultivation and IoT greenhouse sensors

## Investment Thesis Notes

The cold chain gap is the most immediate near-term opportunity with manageable ticket sizes. The data strongly suggests cold chain logistics as one of the four highest-priority near-term sectors, offering the most immediate returns.

The combination of 0% corporate tax on agri-farming, 200% capital allowances, and virtually zero competition in the North creates an exceptional incentive structure. Only ~40 AgriTech startups serve the entire country (just 4 funded), against $1.3B+ in multilateral capital with explicit AgriTech co-investment mandates actively seeking private-sector partners.

The palmyrah value chain modernisation (322% export growth, 115 million trees, 12,000+ workers, ~800 products) and Jaffna salinity crisis (43% of paddy land permanently abandoned) represent clear commercial pathways with diaspora market connections. The $200 million annual post-harvest loss figure alone dwarfs the ticket sizes required for cold storage infrastructure.

Early movers face minimal competition and maximum institutional de-risking support. The convergence of multilateral capital, BOI incentives, and structural undersupply creates a textbook first-mover opportunity.
