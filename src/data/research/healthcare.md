## Why Now

### Macro Tailwind - Donor Capital Flows

A wave of multilateral health financing is converging on Sri Lanka at the exact moment when the country's health workforce crisis - accelerated by post-2022 brain drain - has made technology-enabled care delivery not optional but structurally necessary. Between June 2024 and September 2025, approximately $275 million in new health sector financing was approved by the World Bank, ADB, and the Pandemic Fund, all covering Northern and Eastern Province districts. This donor capital is not general budget support: it carries explicit mandates for health system digitalisation, procurement modernisation, disease surveillance, and primary care facility upgrades across 1,000+ institutions. For HealthTech ventures, this creates procurement budgets, digital infrastructure requirements, and institutional partnerships in a region where the need is acute and competition is minimal.

### Data Point 1: ~$275 Million in Multilateral Health Financing (2024–2025)

~$275 million in multilateral health financing approved in 2024–2025, covering all Northern/Eastern districts with explicit digital health mandates:

- **World Bank PHSEP (Primary Healthcare System Enhancing Project)**: $150 million (approved June 2024). Covers 100% of primary care institutions across all districts - including Jaffna, Kilinochchi, Mullaitivu, Vavuniya, Mannar, Batticaloa, Trincomalee, and Ampara. Disbursement schedule: $10M (FY2025) ramping to $50M (FY2028). Covers all districts including mental health.
- **ADB Strengthening Integrated Health Care Programme**: $106.9 million (approved September 2025). Explicitly funds digitalisation of government health procurement and disease surveillance systems.
- **Pandemic Fund Grant**: $18.4 million (October 2024). Supports laboratory capacity and health workforce development.

(Sources: World Bank Press Release, June 2024; ADB News Release, September 2025; WHO Sri Lanka, October 2024)

### Data Point 2: 58.8% Mental Health Disorder Prevalence

58.8% of conflict-affected adults in Northern Province screen positive for mental health disorders, against a backdrop of just 1 Mental Health Officer per 30+ villages - the largest quantified digital health demand gap in the region.

A cross-sectional study (COMGAP-S) of 1,015 internally displaced adults across all 5 Northern Province districts found:
- Anxiety prevalence: **46.7%**
- Depression: **41.1%**
- PTSD: **13.7%**
- **42.4%** screened positive for two or more disorders

Northern Province depression prevalence at primary care level (4.5%) is nearly double the national rate (2.4%), while the suicide rate (30 per 100,000) is double the national average. Nationally, high mental health difficulties doubled from 2.9% to 6.1% between 2018–2022.

(Sources: Doherty et al., BMC Psychiatry 2019; Senarath et al., BMC Psychiatry 2014; Deivanayagam et al., NIHR Global Health Research 2024; Groundviews, June 2024)

### Data Point 3: Medical Brain Drain - Full Detail

126 specialists and 860 Medical Officers emigrated in 2023 alone - representing attrition rates of ~6.1% and ~4.1% respectively - with Northern Province hospitals closing surgical units due to staff shortages. Approximately 1,000 of Sri Lanka's 3,700 medical specialists have left the country since the crisis began (27% attrition). Mullaitivu Hospital's surgical unit was shuttered entirely for lack of a surgeon.

Pre-crisis staffing was already critical: only 1.2 doctors and 2.4 nurses per 1,000 population at primary care institutions, well below WHO's 4.45 threshold for minimum health worker density. This workforce collapse makes telemedicine, AI-assisted diagnostics, and remote specialist consultations structurally essential rather than merely convenient.

1,700+ medical officers left Sri Lanka during 2022–2024 in total. Brain drain in medical graduates is extreme: 80–90% of medical graduates emigrate permanently.

(Sources: WHO Sri Lanka HRH Country Profile 2024; World Bank Project Appraisal Document P181564, 2024; UK Home Office Country Information Note, December 2024)

### Opportunity Window

Over $275 million in fresh multilateral health capital is flowing into Sri Lanka through 2028 with explicit digital health mandates, just as the post-crisis medical brain drain (27% of specialists lost, hospital units closing in Tamil districts) has made technology-enabled care delivery structurally necessary rather than optional. HealthTech ventures aligned with donor digitalisation requirements can access both government procurement channels and donor-funded implementation budgets in Northern/Eastern provinces where mental health disorder prevalence exceeds 58%, competition is minimal, and the Tamil diaspora provides a natural bridge for technology transfer.

## Deep Context

### Specialist Doctor Crisis - Full Detail

Northern Province has just **147 specialists** serving over **1.1 million people** compared to **620 specialists** for Colombo's 2.5 million residents. This gives the Northern Province a specialist-to-population ratio roughly one-fifth of Colombo's.

The single significant private healthcare facility in the entire North is an **80-bed secondary care centre** in Jaffna - while the national private healthcare market is dominated by 5 chains controlling 45% of bed capacity, all concentrated in the Western Province.

1,700+ medical officers left Sri Lanka during 2022–2024. In 2023 alone, 126 specialists and 860 Medical Officers emigrated (~6.1% and ~4.1% attrition rates). Approximately 1,000 of 3,700 medical specialists have left since the crisis. The surgical unit at Mullaitivu Hospital closed entirely for lack of a surgeon.

**Psychiatrist distribution**: 60% of all psychiatrists are concentrated in the Western Province, leaving Kilinochchi, Mullaitivu, and Mannar with **zero consultant psychiatrists** at various points. Nationally, only one psychiatrist serves per 500,000 people, and only 20% of those with mental illness receive any treatment.

Pre-crisis staffing was already critical: only 1.2 doctors and 2.4 nurses per 1,000 population at primary care institutions, well below WHO's 4.45 threshold.

### COMGAP-S Study Findings - Complete Detail

A landmark COMGAP-S study - a cross-sectional study of 1,015 internally displaced adults across all five Northern districts - found:

- **58.8%** of primary care attendees screened positive for at least one mental health disorder
- **42.4%** screened positive for **two or more** disorders
- **PTSD**: 13.7%
- **Depression**: 41.1%
- **Anxiety**: 46.7%

There are **zero clinical psychologists** in Northern Sri Lanka.

Northern Province depression prevalence at primary care level (4.5%) is nearly double the national rate (2.4%). The suicide rate in Northern Province (30 per 100,000) is double the national average. Nationally, high mental health difficulties doubled from 2.9% to 6.1% between 2018–2022.

### Broader Collective Trauma - Full Detail

A broader collective trauma study across five war-affected districts found even higher rates:
- **PTSD**: 46.8%
- **Depression**: 57.1%

**One in four children** in the Northeast meets PTSD diagnostic criteria.

These figures represent the cumulative impact of a 26-year civil war and its aftermath on an entire population. The mental health crisis in Tamil-majority regions is not a niche concern but a population-level public health emergency.

### Maternal, Infant, and Under-Five Mortality by District - Full Detail

- **Jaffna district**: Highest maternal mortality rate in Sri Lanka at **42.9 per 100,000 live births** in 2019 - nearly double the national average of 22.6
- **Batticaloa**: Infant mortality at **11.0 per 1,000** versus the national 7.4
- **Kilinochchi**: Under-five mortality reached **44 per 1,000** in the 2016 DHS

These figures place Tamil-majority districts at the extreme end of Sri Lanka's health outcome spectrum, comparable to much lower-income countries.

### CKDu (Chronic Kidney Disease of Unknown Etiology) - Full Detail

Chronic kidney disease of unknown etiology (CKDu) is spreading from North Central Province into farming communities in Trincomalee and Northern Province. The scale is significant:
- Over **50,000** estimated patients
- Approximately **10,500 annual deaths**

CKDu is linked to agricultural communities and water quality, making it a cross-cutting health and agriculture issue for the Northern and Eastern Provinces.

### Child Malnutrition (Hill Country/Estate Sector) - Full Detail

In the plantation/Hill Country areas, child malnutrition is at crisis levels:
- Nuwara Eliya's stunting rate: **32–33%** (double the national average)
- **40.3%** overall malnutrition among estate children aged 6–59 months
- Estate workers earn just Rs. 1,700/day (~$5.72) and live in 10×10 foot colonial-era line rooms

These figures represent one of the most acute child malnutrition crises in South Asia outside of conflict zones.

### National Healthcare Market - Full Detail

- National healthcare market: **$3.4–3.5 billion** (4.07% of GDP), growing
- Digital health segment: Projected to reach **$115.4 million** by 2029 at **7.96% CAGR**
- Medical devices market: Reaching **$450 million** by 2030
- National healthcare spend remains low relative to need; the private sector gap in Tamil-majority regions is extreme

### Medical Tourism Corridor Potential

Jaffna's proximity to Tamil Nadu (83 million people, just 50km across the Palk Strait) creates a natural medical tourism corridor once infrastructure catches up. India provided 416,974 tourist arrivals in 2024 (largest source market), and healthcare is a potential dimension of this traffic.

### Telemedicine Context - Full Detail

The government published its first telemedicine guidelines in 2024 - this is a recent regulatory enabler. Sri Lanka has **129 HealthTech startups** (Tracxn data), but **all are Colombo-centric**. No HealthTech startup specifically targets Tamil-majority regions, and **no Tamil-language digital mental health platform exists** - representing a massive gap given the 58.8% mental health disorder prevalence in the very population that speaks Tamil as its primary language.

## Key Organisations & Projects

- **oDoc** - 200,000+ users, teleconsultation platform. Colombo-based, market leader in telemedicine. Does not specifically target Tamil-majority regions.
- **Flash Health** - Cashless outpatient care platform; $200K seed round in January 2026. Colombo-based.
- **Arogya Life Systems** - Smart hospital management platform deployed at 20+ hospitals including Hemas, Durdans, and Jaffna's Venus Hospital. Won "Asia's Best Innovative Healthcare Company Award." Jaffna-origin, emerged from the Yarl Geek Challenge ecosystem. One of the few HealthTech companies with any Northern Province presence.
- **IMHO (International Medical Health Organisation)** - Diaspora-founded organisation. Has invested over $15 million in health projects across Northern Sri Lanka, including building kidney transplant capacity at Jaffna Teaching Hospital. Represents the most significant diaspora health investment to date.
- **Asiri Laboratories** - Only major private diagnostic chain with any Northern presence - limited to collection centres in Jaffna and Batticaloa. All other major chains are entirely Western Province focused.
- **World Bank PHSEP** - $150 million Primary Healthcare System Enhancing Project (approved June 2024). Covers 100% of primary care institutions across all districts including mental health. Disbursements: $10M (FY2025) through $50M (FY2028).
- **ADB Strengthening Integrated Health Care** - $106.9 million (approved September 2025). Explicitly funds digitalisation of government health procurement and disease surveillance systems.
- **Pandemic Fund** - $18.4 million grant (October 2024) for laboratory capacity and health workforce development.
- **SLMDA UK (Sri Lankan Medical and Dental Association UK)** - Established 1982. Key professional body for the UK Tamil medical diaspora - represents a natural bridge for technology transfer and specialist telemedicine.

## Investment Thesis Notes

Specialist healthcare is identified as one of the four highest-priority near-term sectors alongside cold chain logistics, ethical fintech, and aquaculture.

The combination of 58.8% mental health disorder prevalence, zero clinical psychologists in Northern Province, zero Tamil-language digital mental health platforms, and $275M in multilateral health financing with explicit digital mandates creates one of the clearest investment cases across all eight sectors. BOI's zero-minimum-investment threshold for healthcare removes capital barriers to entry entirely.

Tamil-language HealthTech - particularly telemedicine and digital mental health tools - addresses a 58.8% prevalence rate with zero existing digital solutions. The 129 HealthTech startups in Sri Lanka are all Colombo-centric - none targets the Tamil-majority regions where need is most acute.

The Tamil diaspora's heavy professional representation in medicine (via SLMDA UK and equivalent bodies in Canada and Australia) provides a natural technology transfer bridge. Diaspora clinicians can provide remote consultations, training, and clinical governance - a model that addresses both the brain drain (80–90% of medical graduates emigrate) and the specialist shortage simultaneously.

The $275M in donor capital creates procurement budgets and digital infrastructure requirements that HealthTech ventures can serve. The PHSEP's coverage of 100% of primary care institutions across all districts including mental health creates a platform for technology deployment at scale.
